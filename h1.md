## b) Review of the review

- What are the key areas being researched?
    - Six categories of social engineering countermeasures, including employee training, technical controls and security policies
    - Countermeasures across three phases: before attack, during attack and after attack
    - Combining different measures into a coordinated security strategy
- What's widely agreed upon?
    - Not a single countermeasure is sufficient alone, multiple measures are needed
    - Employee training and technical controls are both important
    - Organizations should check if their security measures work and learn from past attack
- What are the open questions?
    - How can organizations learn better from incidents?
    - How effective are AI-based detection methods?
    - How can different security measures work better together?
 
## c) Threat modeling quickrun

- Company:
    - An IT company that provides software for business customers
- Crown jewels:
    - Customer data, business continuity, money, company reputation
- Bad guys:
    - Cybercriminals who want money and confidential customer data. They try to get it with phishing emails, stolen credentials and ransomware
- Why us? (COI)
    - Capability: They have the tools and the desire to attack IT systems
    - Opportunity: Employees may fall for phishing emails. IT companies employ many people other than rock-solid IT professionals, such as those in finance and payroll, but even IT professional may fall for one
- Risks
    - Risk 1: Ransomware attack: likelihood 30 %, impact 300k €, estimated risk = 90k €
    - Risk 2: Customer data breach: likelihood 15 %, impact 150k €, estimated risk = 22,5k €
    - Biggest risk: Ransomware attack
- What shall we do with biggest risk (META)?
    - Mitigate: Reduce the risk by implementing MFA, creating regular OFFLINE backups, keeping software updated and employee training for recognizing phishing emails
